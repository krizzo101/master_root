# Stage 1: Requirements & Analysis - Auto-Coding Solution

## Overview

The Requirements & Analysis stage establishes the foundation for a successful auto-coding solution by systematically identifying, gathering, and validating all necessary requirements while assessing technical and business feasibility.

## Objectives

### Primary Objectives
1. **Comprehensive Requirement Identification** - Discover and document all functional, non-functional, and technical requirements
2. **Stakeholder Alignment** - Ensure all key stakeholders are identified and their needs understood
3. **Feasibility Validation** - Confirm technical and business viability of the auto-coding solution
4. **Risk Assessment** - Identify potential challenges and mitigation strategies early
5. **Success Criteria Definition** - Establish measurable outcomes for the entire project

### Secondary Objectives
1. **Technology Stack Evaluation** - Assess and select appropriate tools, frameworks, and platforms
2. **Resource Planning** - Determine human, technical, and financial resources required
3. **Timeline Estimation** - Provide realistic project duration estimates
4. **Quality Standards Definition** - Establish coding standards, testing requirements, and acceptance criteria

## Deliverables

### 1. Stakeholder Analysis Document
**Format:** Structured document with stakeholder matrix
**Content:**
- Primary stakeholders (decision makers, end users, technical leads)
- Secondary stakeholders (affected teams, compliance, security)
- Stakeholder influence/interest mapping
- Communication preferences and schedules
- Approval authority matrix

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- All stakeholder types identified and categorized
- Contact information and roles documented
- Decision-making hierarchy clearly defined
- Sign-off from project sponsor obtained

### 2. Functional Requirements Specification
**Format:** Structured requirements document with traceability matrix
**Content:**
- Core auto-coding functionality requirements
- User story definitions with acceptance criteria
- Input/output specifications
- Business rule definitions
- Integration requirements with existing systems
- User interface and experience requirements

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Each requirement uniquely identified and traceable
- Requirements written in testable format
- Priority levels assigned (Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, Won't Have)
- Dependencies between requirements mapped
- Requirements reviewed and approved by stakeholders

### 3. Non-Functional Requirements Document
**Format:** Technical specification document
**Content:**
- Performance requirements (response times, throughput, scalability)
- Security requirements (authentication, authorization, data protection)
- Reliability and availability targets
- Usability and accessibility standards
- Compliance requirements (regulatory, organizational)
- Maintenance and support requirements

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Quantifiable metrics defined for each non-functional requirement
- Testing strategies outlined for validation
- Risk assessment completed for each requirement
- Technical feasibility confirmed by architecture team

### 4. Technical Feasibility Analysis
**Format:** Technical assessment report with recommendations
**Content:**
- Technology stack evaluation and recommendations
- Architecture options analysis
- Third-party integration assessment
- Development tool and platform evaluation
- Scalability and performance analysis
- Security architecture evaluation

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Multiple architecture options evaluated with pros/cons
- Recommended technology stack with justification
- Proof-of-concept results for critical components
- Risk assessment with mitigation strategies
- Cost-benefit analysis completed

### 5. Business Feasibility Study
**Format:** Business case document with financial analysis
**Content:**
- Cost-benefit analysis
- Return on investment (ROI) calculations
- Resource requirement assessment
- Timeline and milestone definitions
- Risk analysis and mitigation strategies
- Success metrics and KPIs

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Financial projections validated by finance team
- Resource availability confirmed
- Timeline approved by stakeholders
- Risk register populated with mitigation plans
- Business case approved by executive sponsors

### 6. Project Charter
**Format:** Executive summary document
**Content:**
- Project vision and objectives
- Scope definition and boundaries
- High-level timeline and milestones
- Resource allocation and budget approval
- Success criteria and acceptance conditions
- Governance structure and reporting requirements

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Signed approval from all key stakeholders
- Clear scope boundaries established
- Budget and resource allocation approved
- Success criteria measurable and time-bound
- Governance structure defined and accepted

## Success Criteria

### Quantitative Success Metrics

1. **Requirements Completeness**
   - 100% of identified stakeholders interviewed
   - ≥95% of requirements traceable to business needs
   - Zero critical requirements discovered after Stage 1 completion
   - Requirements stability index ≥90% (minimal changes after approval)

2. **Stakeholder Satisfaction**
   - ≥90% stakeholder approval rating on requirements accuracy
   - 100% sign-off from primary stakeholders
   - Zero unresolved conflicts between stakeholder requirements
   - Communication effectiveness rating ≥4.5/5

3. **Technical Feasibility Validation**
   - ≥3 architecture options evaluated with documented trade-offs
   - Proof-of-concept success rate 100% for critical components
   - Technical risk assessment completeness 100%
   - Performance target feasibility confirmed for all requirements

4. **Business Case Validation**
   - ROI calculation accuracy within ±10% confidence interval
   - Cost estimation accuracy within ±15% of final budget
   - Resource availability confirmed for ≥90% of project duration
   - Timeline estimation accuracy within ±20% of actual delivery

### Qualitative Success Indicators

1. **Strategic Alignment**
   - Solution directly supports organizational strategic objectives
   - Clear value proposition articulated and accepted
   - Competitive advantage opportunities identified
   - Innovation potential quantified and validated

2. **Risk Management Maturity**
   - Comprehensive risk register with likelihood and impact assessments
   - Mitigation strategies defined for all high and medium risks
   - Contingency plans established for critical path activities
   - Risk ownership assigned and accepted

3. **Quality Framework Establishment**
   - Coding standards defined and agreed upon
   - Testing strategy comprehensive and realistic
   - Acceptance criteria unambiguous and testable
   - Quality gates defined for each subsequent stage

4. **Communication Excellence**
   - Clear, consistent messaging across all stakeholder groups
   - Effective feedback loops established
   - Conflict resolution mechanisms defined
   - Regular communication schedule established and maintained

## Stage Completion Gates

### Gate 1: Stakeholder Validation
**Trigger:** All stakeholders identified and initial requirements gathered
**Criteria:**
- Stakeholder analysis approved by project sponsor
- Initial requirements list compiled and reviewed
- Communication plan established and activated
- Conflict identification process completed

**Approval Required From:**
- Project Sponsor
- Primary Business Stakeholders
- Technical Lead

### Gate 2: Requirements Finalization
**Trigger:** All requirements documented and technically assessed
**Criteria:**
- Requirements specification complete and reviewed
- Technical feasibility confirmed for all requirements
- Requirements prioritization completed and approved
- Traceability matrix established and validated

**Approval Required From:**
- Business Analyst Lead
- Technical Architecture Team
- Quality Assurance Lead
- Primary End User Representatives

### Gate 3: Business Case Approval
**Trigger:** Complete feasibility analysis and business case prepared
**Criteria:**
- ROI analysis completed and validated
- Resource requirements confirmed and committed
- Timeline and budget approved
- Risk assessment completed with mitigation plans

**Approval Required From:**
- Executive Sponsor
- Finance Team
- Resource Managers
- Risk Management Committee

### Gate 4: Project Charter Authorization
**Trigger:** All previous gates passed and project ready for execution planning
**Criteria:**
- Project charter drafted and reviewed
- Success criteria defined and agreed upon
- Governance structure established
- Stage 2 readiness confirmed

**Approval Required From:**
- Executive Steering Committee
- All Primary Stakeholders
- Project Management Office (if applicable)

## Dependencies and Prerequisites

### Incoming Dependencies
- Organizational strategy and objectives clearly defined
- Initial problem statement or opportunity identification
- Executive sponsorship secured
- Basic resource allocation for analysis phase approved

### Outgoing Dependencies
- Stage 2 (System Architecture) cannot begin until technical feasibility confirmed
- Stage 3 (Development Planning) requires finalized requirements and approved architecture
- Resource procurement dependent on business case approval
- Team assembly requires approved project charter

## Risk Factors and Mitigation

### High-Risk Areas
1. **Incomplete Stakeholder Identification**
   - Risk: Missing critical requirements from unidentified stakeholders
   - Mitigation: Multi-channel stakeholder discovery process, escalation paths

2. **Requirements Volatility**
   - Risk: Continuous requirement changes affecting project stability
   - Mitigation: Change control process, requirements freezing at gate points

3. **Technical Feasibility Overconfidence**
   - Risk: Underestimating technical complexity leading to project failure
   - Mitigation: Conservative estimates, mandatory proof-of-concepts, expert reviews

4. **Business Case Optimism**
   - Risk: Inflated benefits or understated costs leading to project failure
   - Mitigation: Independent validation, conservative estimates, regular reviews

## Success Handoff to Stage 2

Stage 1 is considered successfully complete when:
1. All deliverables approved and signed off
2. All success criteria met with documented evidence
3. All completion gates passed with required approvals
4. Stage 2 team briefed and ready to proceed
5. Requirements baseline established and locked
6. Communication channels and governance structure operational

The handoff to Stage 2 (System Architecture) includes transfer of all documentation, stakeholder contacts, approved requirements baseline, and any constraints or assumptions that will guide the architectural design process.
