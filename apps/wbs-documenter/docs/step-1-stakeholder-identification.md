# Step 1: Stakeholder Identification

## Overview

Stakeholder identification is the critical first step in the WBS documentation process for auto-coding solutions. This step establishes the foundation for all subsequent analysis by identifying, categorizing, and engaging the right people who will influence, contribute to, or be affected by the auto-coding implementation.

## Objectives

### Primary Objectives
1. **Complete Stakeholder Mapping**: Identify all individuals, teams, and organizations that have a stake in the auto-coding solution
2. **Stakeholder Categorization**: Classify stakeholders by influence, impact, and engagement requirements
3. **Engagement Strategy Development**: Define how each stakeholder group will be involved throughout the project
4. **Communication Framework Establishment**: Create channels and protocols for ongoing stakeholder interaction

### Secondary Objectives
- Minimize project risks through comprehensive stakeholder analysis
- Establish buy-in and support from key decision makers
- Create feedback loops for iterative improvement
- Ensure compliance with organizational governance requirements

## Stakeholder Categories

### 1. Primary Stakeholders (Direct Impact/High Influence)

#### Executive Sponsors
- **Role**: Provide strategic direction and funding
- **Identification Method**: Review organizational chart for C-level executives and VPs overseeing technology initiatives
- **Key Characteristics**: Budget authority, strategic decision-making power, success metrics ownership
- **Engagement Strategy**: Executive briefings, milestone reviews, strategic alignment sessions

#### Product Owners/Business Owners
- **Role**: Define business requirements and success criteria
- **Identification Method**: Identify business unit leaders who will benefit from or be affected by automation
- **Key Characteristics**: Domain expertise, business process ownership, ROI accountability
- **Engagement Strategy**: Requirements workshops, regular demos, feedback sessions

#### Development Team Leads
- **Role**: Technical implementation oversight
- **Identification Method**: Current development team structure, architect roles, technical leads
- **Key Characteristics**: Technical decision-making authority, team management, implementation responsibility
- **Engagement Strategy**: Technical design reviews, implementation planning, resource allocation discussions

### 2. Secondary Stakeholders (Indirect Impact/Medium Influence)

#### End Users
- **Role**: Will interact with or be replaced by the auto-coding solution
- **Identification Method**: User research, workflow analysis, job role mapping
- **Key Characteristics**: Daily operational impact, workflow dependencies, training needs
- **Engagement Strategy**: User interviews, prototype testing, change management support

#### IT Operations/DevOps Teams
- **Role**: Deployment, maintenance, and operational support
- **Identification Method**: Infrastructure team rosters, deployment pipeline owners
- **Key Characteristics**: System reliability responsibility, deployment expertise, operational knowledge
- **Engagement Strategy**: Technical workshops, deployment planning, operational readiness reviews

#### Quality Assurance Teams
- **Role**: Testing strategy and validation
- **Identification Method**: QA team structure, test automation specialists
- **Key Characteristics**: Quality standards ownership, testing methodology expertise
- **Engagement Strategy**: Test planning sessions, quality criteria definition, validation protocols

#### Compliance/Security Teams
- **Role**: Ensure regulatory and security requirements are met
- **Identification Method**: Review security organizational structure, compliance officers
- **Key Characteristics**: Risk assessment expertise, regulatory knowledge, approval authority
- **Engagement Strategy**: Security reviews, compliance checkpoints, risk assessment sessions

### 3. Tertiary Stakeholders (Indirect Impact/Lower Influence)

#### Training Teams
- **Role**: Develop training materials and support user adoption
- **Identification Method**: Learning and development department, technical writers
- **Key Characteristics**: Training expertise, documentation skills, change management support
- **Engagement Strategy**: Training needs analysis, documentation reviews, adoption metrics

#### Support Teams
- **Role**: Provide ongoing user support and troubleshooting
- **Identification Method**: Help desk organization, support ticket systems
- **Key Characteristics**: User support expertise, troubleshooting skills, escalation protocols
- **Engagement Strategy**: Support documentation, troubleshooting guides, escalation procedures

#### Procurement/Legal Teams
- **Role**: Handle vendor relationships and legal compliance
- **Identification Method**: Procurement department, legal counsel assignments
- **Key Characteristics**: Contract expertise, vendor management, legal compliance
- **Engagement Strategy**: Vendor evaluation, contract reviews, legal compliance validation

```
│                 │
```

**RACI Categories:**

- **Consulted**: Who provides input
- **Informed**: Who needs to be updated

## Engagement Strategies

### High-Influence/High-Impact Stakeholders
- **Frequency**: Weekly to bi-weekly touchpoints
- **Format**: Executive briefings, strategic reviews, decision points
- **Content**: Strategic alignment, resource requirements, risk mitigation
- **Success Metrics**: Decision velocity, resource allocation, strategic support

### Medium-Influence/Medium-Impact Stakeholders
- **Frequency**: Bi-weekly to monthly touchpoints
- **Format**: Working sessions, technical reviews, progress updates
- **Content**: Technical details, implementation progress, operational impacts
- **Success Metrics**: Technical quality, operational readiness, requirement satisfaction

### Low-Influence/Low-Impact Stakeholders
- **Frequency**: Monthly to quarterly touchpoints
- **Format**: Status updates, training sessions, documentation reviews
- **Content**: Implementation status, training needs, support requirements
- **Success Metrics**: User adoption, support effectiveness, training completion

## Communication Framework

### 1. Communication Channels

#### Executive Level
- **Channel**: Executive dashboards, board presentations
- **Frequency**: Monthly executive reviews
- **Content**: Strategic progress, ROI metrics, risk status
- **Owner**: Project sponsor/executive champion

#### Operational Level
- **Channel**: Project management tools, regular meetings
- **Frequency**: Weekly status updates
- **Content**: Implementation progress, technical issues, resource needs
- **Owner**: Project manager/technical lead

#### Working Level
- **Channel**: Collaboration tools, technical forums
- **Frequency**: Daily standups, sprint reviews
- **Content**: Technical details, implementation challenges, solutions
- **Owner**: Development team leads

### 2. Escalation Protocols

```
┌─ Escalation Path ─┐
│                   │
│ Level 1: Team     │
│ Lead Resolution   │
│        ↓          │
│ Level 2: Project  │
│ Manager Review    │
│        ↓          │

│                   │
```

**Escalation Criteria:**
- **Level 1**: Technical implementation issues

## Success Criteria

### Identification Completeness
- **Target**: 100% of relevant stakeholders identified
- **Measurement**: Stakeholder registry completeness validated by multiple sources
- **Validation**: Cross-reference with organizational charts, process maps, and subject matter expert interviews

### Engagement Quality
- **Target**: 90% stakeholder participation in initial engagement activities
- **Measurement**: Participation rates in workshops, interviews, and feedback sessions
- **Validation**: Stakeholder feedback surveys and engagement metrics

### Communication Effectiveness
- **Target**: 85% stakeholder satisfaction with communication frequency and content
- **Measurement**: Regular stakeholder satisfaction surveys
- **Validation**: Feedback incorporation rates and communication effectiveness metrics

## Risk Mitigation

### Common Stakeholder Identification Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Missing key stakeholders | High | Multiple identification methods, cross-validation |
| Stakeholder disengagement | Medium | Clear value proposition, regular touchpoints |
| Communication breakdowns | High | Multiple channels, regular feedback loops |
| Conflicting priorities | Medium | Early alignment sessions, clear prioritization |

### Mitigation Tactics

1. **Multi-Source Validation**: Use multiple identification methods to ensure completeness
2. **Stakeholder Champions**: Identify and cultivate champions in each stakeholder group
3. **Regular Assessment**: Quarterly review and update of stakeholder analysis
4. **Flexible Engagement**: Adapt engagement strategies based on stakeholder feedback

## Integration with WBS Process

### Input to Subsequent Steps
- **Step 2 (Requirements Gathering)**: Stakeholder registry provides interview targets and requirement sources
- **Step 3 (Technical Analysis)**: Technical stakeholders inform architecture and implementation constraints
- **Step 4 (Risk Assessment)**: Stakeholder concerns and resistance points inform risk identification
- **Step 5 (Implementation Planning)**: Stakeholder availability and capacity inform resource planning

### Continuous Process
Stakeholder identification is not a one-time activity but an ongoing process that should be updated throughout the project lifecycle as new stakeholders are discovered or organizational changes occur.
